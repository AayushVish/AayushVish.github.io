PROVIENT

A luxury recommerce platform where every object carries a verified history.

Duration: 4 weeks

OVERVIEW

Buying pre-owned luxury comes with one major challenge: trust. Provient helps buyers make confident decisions through transparent product history and verified ownership records. I designed the end-to-end product experience, from discovery to purchase.

PROBLEM

The Badge Problem

Platforms reduce trust to a single “Authenticated” checkmark. It answers “is it real?” but not “where has it been?”

The Grid Problem

Most luxury resale platforms use product grids like fast-fashion sites. You cannot feel the weight of provenance in a thumbnail.

The Receipt Problem

After purchase, buyers get a transaction confirmation. Provient should make the transfer of custodianship feel like a ceremony.

THE PROCESS

Research

Analysed eight competing platforms—TheRealReal, Vestiaire Collective, Chrono24, Rebag, Fashionphile, eBay, Farfetch and StockX—mapping authentication, provenance display and post-purchase experience. The consistent finding: provenance was treated as a compliance requirement, not a product feature.

What competitors got right

Authentication rigour, escrow protection, condition grading, and search/filtering.

The gap none filled

Ownership history as a discoverable feature, geographic journey, service/restoration as story, and post-purchase as a legacy moment.

Information Architecture

The platform is structured around four buyer-facing entry points—Explore, Categories, Stories and Archive—with Sell as a separate seller mode. The navigation reflects the platform’s dual identity: marketplace and editorial archive.

SCREENS & DESIGN DECISIONS

Homepage — Hero

The hero leads with “The stories live on”—a single curated item, not a carousel of bestsellers. Story is the discovery hook, not the product spec.

Curator Picks

An oversized editorial card integrates provenance before the price, signalling that narrative precedes commerce.

Auction Section

The countdown timer is typographically restrained. Urgency lives in scarcity language, not visual noise.

Journey as Subtitle

“Paris → Milan → Tokyo” sits directly beneath the item name. Geographic journey is identity and answers the buyer’s first unspoken question.

Trust Strip

Verification signals sit below price and CTAs. Desire first, reassurance second—so the page feels desirable before it feels defensive.

Story as Quote

The item narrative gets the same visual weight as the price, treating story as the emotional core rather than supporting copy.

Source of Verification

Each timeline entry lists source materials such as archive photographs, sale receipts and handwritten notes, turning provenance into visible evidence.

Confidence Score

Verified entries surface granular confidence so buyers can understand that a blockchain timestamp and a handwritten note carry different evidentiary weight.

Delivery Stages

“Preparing for shipment”, “White-glove delivery”, “Delivery & inspection” and “Ownership Celebration” make the functional flow feel like a narrative.

No Continue Shopping

Post-purchase CTAs point into ownership—digital certificate and collection—rather than immediate repurchase.

Certificate

The certificate of ownership surfaces directly on confirmation, giving the buyer proof of legacy in the same moment as the order.

Digital Passport

The seller is positioned as a narrator, not a vendor. “Your Story” appears early in the listing flow because provenance is Provient’s differentiating asset.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Make provenance feel like part of the product.

The interface treats history, evidence and ownership as experiences to discover—not compliance badges to tolerate. That shift gives luxury recommerce a visual language closer to an archive than a marketplace.
